About Me

I’ve always been curious about how systems work, especially the ones people depend on every day. That curiosity usually turns into problem solving for me: figuring out what’s not working, why it’s not working, and how to make it better.

I studied computer science in college and found myself drawn to both the structure of development and the flexibility of design. Over time, I realized I wasn’t interested in tech for tech’s sake. I was most interested in work that intersected with care, communication, access, and real human problems.

I currently work as a Technical Solutions Specialist at IEEE, supporting technical needs across Student & Academic programs. My work spans web technology, data, automation, reporting, troubleshooting, and digital strategy, often sitting between program needs and technical implementation. Before IEEE, I worked in healthcare and behavioral health technology, including remote patient monitoring, where I supported systems and helped bridge gaps between clinicians, platforms, and patients.

I’ve also built independent projects that combine technology with mental health education, including a public-facing DBT flashcards website designed around accessibility and visual clarity. Outside of development, I make expressive illustrations, digital work with hand-drawn elements, and small sculptural pieces that explore mental health themes. Art gives me another way to make complicated ideas feel warmer and more personal.

My interest in problem solving and mental health also led me to complete peer support training and become a Certified Recovery Support Practitioner. Whether I’m debugging a technical problem, designing a digital resource, or supporting someone directly, I’m drawn to work that makes systems easier to navigate and helps people feel understood, supported, and seen.
